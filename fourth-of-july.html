Fourth of July: BBQ, Games, and Fireworks Safety

Menu ideas, fireworks safety, parade tips, sun safety, and backyard games the whole family can play.

The Fourth of July is one big outdoor party. Burgers, swimming, fireworks. But with kids, there are real safety things to know. Here's how to enjoy the day without anyone ending up in the ER.

Part 1

Backyard BBQ Menu

The Classic Spread

  • Main: Burgers and hot dogs. Grill them ahead, keep warm in a cooler.
  • Sides: Coleslaw, potato salad, chips and dip. Make the day before.
  • Dessert: Fruit, popsicles, or store-bought cookie platter.
  • Drinks: Lemonade, iced tea, and plenty of water. It's hot out.
Part 2

Fireworks Safety (Read This First)

Essential Rules

  • Skip personal fireworks with young kids. Loud noises scare them, and sparklers burn at 2,000°F. Watch a professional show instead.
  • Stay back. If you do attend a public show, set up at least 500 feet from the launch site. Bring ear protection for babies.
  • Have water ready. A bucket of water and a hose near any outdoor fire is non-negotiable.
  • Glow sticks instead of sparklers. Kids get the light effect with zero burn risk.
  • Check local laws. Many cities ban consumer fireworks. Don't risk a fine.
Part 3

Sun and Heat Safety

July 4th is hot. Kids burn faster than adults.

  • Sunscreen: SPF 30+, reapply every 2 hours. Don't forget ears, feet, and the back of the neck.
  • Hydration: Water every hour, even if they don't ask. Bring more than you think you need.
  • Shade: Set up a pop-up canopy or umbrella. Kids need a place to cool off.
  • Timing: Plan outdoor activities for morning or late afternoon. The middle of the day is for the pool or AC.
  • Watch for heat exhaustion: Dizziness, nausea, or rapid breathing means get inside immediately.
Part 4

Backyard Games for All Ages

  • Cornhole: The default backyard game. Buy a set, play in teams.
  • Water balloon toss: Fill balloons the night before. Kids will play for an hour.
  • Sack race: Old pillowcases. Hilarious, low cost.
  • Sidewalk chalk: American flag, stars, whatever. Toddlers can join.
Part 5

Going to a Parade? Here's What to Bring

  • Blankets or folding chairs. Parades last 1-2 hours.
  • A bag for candy and beads. Parades throw lots of stuff.
  • Sunscreen, hats, and water.
  • Ear protection for babies and toddlers.
  • Arrive 30 minutes early for a good spot.
Part 6

Common Questions

Will fireworks scare my toddler?▾
Likely yes. The booms are loud and sudden. Bring ear protection, a stroller for a quick escape, and leave early if it's too much. You don't have to stay for the whole show.
What time should we go to the fireworks?▾
Arrive 1-2 hours early to get a good spot. Bring a blanket, snacks, and toys. The show itself is usually 15-25 minutes after dark.
Do we need to dress in red, white, and blue?▾
Not required, but kids think it's fun. A red shirt and blue shorts is enough. Nobody will card you for not wearing a flag onesie.
