January 1

New Year's Day with Kids

The calm day after the countdown: easy brunch, kid-friendly resolutions, and low-key activities to start the year together.

New Year's Day (January 1) is the quiet counterpart to New Year's Eve. After the countdown, the confetti, and the late night, most families want one thing: a slow, easy day. Here's how to make it special without adding more stress.

Morning

Easy New Year's Day brunch

No-fuss morning

Skip the restaurants — they're crowded and slow. Make something at home that feels celebratory but doesn't require cooking from scratch:

  • Pancakes or waffles with sprinkles (the easiest way to make breakfast feel like a party)
  • A fruit platter shaped like a clock or "new year"
  • Mimosa for adults, sparkling cider for kids
  • Don't clean up the confetti from last night — let it be part of the morning vibe
Resolutions

Kid-friendly New Year's resolutions

Keep them small and specific

Big goals ("be nicer") don't stick. Make resolutions kids can actually do:

  • "Read one book every week"
  • "Try one new vegetable this month"
  • "Put my shoes away without being asked"
  • "Hug Mom and Dad every morning"

Make a resolution jar

Write each family member's resolution on a slip of paper, fold it, and put it in a decorated jar. Read them next New Year's Eve to see what stuck.

Afternoon

Low-key January 1 activities

What actually works with tired kids

  • A family walk or hike — get some fresh air after being up late
  • Bake cookies or a simple cake together
  • Watch a movie in pajamas (you're all still in them, anyway)
  • Sort through toys and donate what kids have outgrown

Why we keep New Year's Day simple

  • Everyone is tired. The goal is recovery, not another big production.
  • The best New Year's Day traditions are the low-effort ones you'll actually repeat.
  • Let the day be slow. The new year doesn't need to start at 8am sharp.
FAQ

Common questions

Is New Year's Day a federal holiday? +
Yes. January 1 is a federal holiday. Government offices, banks, and many businesses close.
What's the difference between New Year's Eve and New Year's Day? +
New Year's Eve (December 31) is the night of countdowns, parties, and staying up late. New Year's Day (January 1) is the quiet morning after — slow, recovery-focused, and often spent with family.
What if my kids went to bed early on New Year's Eve? +
No problem. New Year's Day is when you celebrate together — brunch, resolutions, and a family activity. You don't need to stay up to midnight to have a good first day of the year.
