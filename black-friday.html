Day after Thanksgiving

Black Friday with Kids

A calm, intentional approach to the biggest shopping weekend — what to buy, what to skip, and how to keep your budget (and your sanity) intact.

Black Friday (the day after Thanksgiving) has become the start of holiday shopping season. For families, it's less about camping outside stores and more about snagging deals on things you already planned to buy — winter coats, kids' gifts, household essentials. The goal: spend less, not buy more.

What to Actually Buy

Deals worth planning for

Kids' big-ticket items

  • Winter coats, snow boots, and kids' clothing — these are often cheapest in late November.
  • Bikes, scooters, and outdoor toys — holiday season pricing beats spring.
  • Games and puzzles for the whole family — use them during winter break.

Household & gifting

  • Small appliances (coffee makers, air fryers) — Black Friday/Cyber Monday is the best time of year.
  • Holiday gifts you were already buying — wrap them now, don't delay.
  • Toys and gifts for family members — compare prices ahead of time.
What to Skip

Deals that aren't worth the stress

Things you don't need

  • "Doorbuster" TV deals if your current TV works — you'll buy a $600 TV to "save" $200.
  • Fast fashion and cheap toys — they break before Christmas is over.
  • Anything with a long return window you won't use.

Crowds and early mornings

With young kids in tow, 5am store runs are rarely worth it. Most Black Friday deals are available online now, and Cyber Monday (the following Monday) often matches or beats them. Shop from your couch in sweatpants.

Family budget approach

  • Make a list before the sales start. Stick to it.
  • Set a total budget. If you're "saving" 50%, you're still spending money — only buy what was on the list.
  • Compare prices now. Retailers often raise prices before Black Friday to make the "sale" look bigger.
  • Use Rakuten or similar cash-back sites — free money on purchases you were already making.
FAQ

Common questions

Is Black Friday the same as Cyber Monday? +
No. Black Friday is the Friday after Thanksgiving (in-store + online). Cyber Monday is the following Monday — online-only deals. Many retailers now run the same sales across both days.
Should I take kids shopping? +
With young kids, probably not. Online shopping is faster, calmer, and you can do it after they're asleep. Save the in-store experience for a regular weekend, not the busiest shopping day of the year.
What if I miss the deals? +
You'll be fine. Post-Christmas sales, Amazon Prime Day, and back-to-school sales all have deep discounts. Missing Black Friday is not a financial disaster.
