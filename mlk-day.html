Third Monday of January

MLK Jr. Day with Kids

A day on, not a day off. Age-appropriate history, kids' books, volunteering ideas, and family-friendly ways to honor Dr. King's legacy.

Martin Luther King Jr. Day (third Monday of January) is more than a day off school. It's a chance to talk about fairness, kindness, and standing up for what's right — in words kids can understand. Here's how to make it meaningful without overwhelming young children.

Age-Appropriate History

How to explain MLK to kids

For younger kids (3–7)

Keep it simple and hopeful: "Dr. King was a man who believed everyone should be treated kindly, no matter what they look like. He helped people get along." Focus on the "I Have a Dream" part, not the hard parts.

For older kids (8+)

You can add context: not long ago, Black and white people weren't allowed to share schools, buses, or water fountains. Dr. King led peaceful protests to change that. Talk about why peaceful protest matters, and why his work isn't finished.

Books

Books to read together

For young kids

  • "A Picture Book of Martin Luther King, Jr." by David A. Adler
  • "Martin's Big Words" by Doreen Rappaport
  • "I Am Martin Luther King, Jr." by Brad Meltzer

For older kids

  • "This Is Your Time" by Ruby Bridges
  • "March" trilogy by John Lewis (graphic novel, for teens)
Volunteering

Make it a day of service

Family-friendly volunteer ideas

  • Sort donations at a local food bank
  • Write thank-you cards to community helpers (teachers, nurses, mail carriers)
  • Clean up a local park or playground
  • Donate books or toys to a children's shelter

What to keep in mind

  • MLK Day is about service, not just a day off. The motto is "a day on, not a day off."
  • Be honest but age-appropriate. Young kids don't need graphic details; older kids can handle more.
  • Talk about what fairness means in your own family — sharing, including everyone, standing up when someone is left out.
FAQ

Common questions

When is MLK Day in 2026? +
MLK Day is always the third Monday of January. In 2026, that's January 19, 2026.
Is MLK Day a federal holiday? +
Yes. It became a federal holiday in 1983, and was first observed in all 50 states in 2000. Government offices, banks, and many schools close.
What if my kids are too young for the history? +
Start with kindness and fairness. Read a picture book, do a small service activity, and talk about why we're nice to everyone. You can add more context as they grow.
