Family Guide · February 14

Valentine's Day for the Whole Family

Crafts, treats, classroom tips, family movie night, and date night ideas that actually work with kids.

Valentine's Day isn't just for couples. It's a chance to tell your people you love them — including the small ones. The best family Valentine's Days are low-key, sweet, and don't involve a reservation you can't get.

Part 1

Easy Valentine Crafts

Heart Suncatchers

Cut hearts from colored tissue paper, glue them between contact paper, and hang in a window. Kids can tear the paper themselves — no precision required.

Handprint Heart Card

Trace both hands on red construction paper, overlap the thumbs to make a heart shape. Write a message inside. It's the card grandparents actually keep.

Paper Chain of Kindness

Cut strips of pink and red paper. On each strip, write one kind thing about a family member. Link them together into a chain and hang it up.

Part 2

Kid-Friendly Treats

Simple Valentine Snacks

  • Heart toast: Use a cookie cutter to cut heart shapes from toast. Add strawberry jam.
  • Strawberry bananas: Dip banana slices in melted chocolate, add sprinkles. Chill 10 minutes.
  • Vegetable hearts: Cut bell peppers and cucumbers into heart shapes. Nobody turns down a fun-shaped veggie.
  • Red velvet pancakes: Add a drop of red food coloring to pancake batter. Heart shapes with a cookie cutter.
Part 3

Classroom Valentine Tips

  • Check the allergy list. Many schools are nut-free now. Stick to non-food Valentines: stickers, pencils, erasers.
  • Buy in bulk. A pack of 30 Valentines at the dollar store beats hand-making them at 10 PM on February 13.
  • Have your kid sign them. Even if it's just a scribble, it's theirs.
  • Don't forget the teacher. A small card or a $5 coffee gift card goes a long way.
Part 4

Family Valentine Movie Night

After the kids are in bed (or while they eat snacks on the couch).

  • Family pick: Lady and the Tramp, Wall-E, or The Princess Bride.
  • Parent pick (after bedtime): Something you actually want to watch. You earned it.
  • Snacks: Popcorn, chocolate-covered strawberries, sparkling cider.
  • Blanket fort: Build one in the living room. Kids will talk about it for weeks.
Part 5

Date Night (Without a Babysitter)

You don't need a fancy restaurant. The point is being together.

  • Order takeout, put the kids to bed, and eat on the couch.
  • After bedtime, have a glass of wine and watch a movie together.
  • Write each other a note. It's cheesy, but it works.
  • If you can swing a babysitter, go to dinner. If not, couch date night is just as good.
Part 6

Common Questions

Should we give our kids Valentine gifts?▾
A small thing is fine — a book, a sticker pack, a box of their favorite snack. You don't need to compete with Christmas. The point is the celebration, not the stuff.
Do we need to do something big?▾
No. The best Valentine's Days are the low-key ones. Pancakes in the morning, a craft after school, and takeout for dinner. Kids remember the moments, not the budget.
What if we're separated or single?▾
Valentine's Day is just as much about kids, grandparents, and friends as it is about couples. Make it a "family love" day. Tell the people in your life you appreciate them.
