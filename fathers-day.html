Father's Day: Do What Dad Actually Likes

Outdoor activities, DIY gifts, BBQ tips, and the truth about what dads really want on their day.

Father's Day is easy if you stop overthinking it. Most dads just want to grill, watch a game, and not be asked for anything for 6 hours. Here's how to give him that.

Part 1

Outdoor Activities Dads Love

Plan the Day Around Him

  • Backyard BBQ: Dad grills, kids play, nobody cooks indoors. Paper plates, lawn chairs, done.
  • Bike ride or walk: Pick a trail, bring snacks. Low pressure, fresh air.
  • Camping in the backyard: Set up a tent, roast hot dogs over the grill, sleep outside if kids are into it.
  • Sports on TV: Put the game on, order pizza, let him watch uninterrupted.
Part 2

DIY Gifts That Don't Suck

"DAD" Photo Collage

Print 4-5 photos of dad with the kids. Arrange them into the letters D-A-D. Frame it. It's cheap, meaningful, and goes on his desk.

Custom Coupon Book

  • One free car wash (by the kids, kind of)
  • One dinner of his choice (no takeout debate)
  • One morning of sleeping in

Painted Mug

Kids decorate a plain ceramic mug with paint markers. It's messy, it's imperfect, he'll use it every morning.

Part 3

BBQ Tips for the Home Cook

  • Prep the night before: marinate meat, chop veggies, make sides.
  • Don't flip the burgers every 30 seconds. Once, maybe twice.
  • Let meat rest 5 minutes after coming off the grill. It matters.
  • Have a backup: hot dogs for kids who won't eat burgers.
  • Keep a spray bottle of water nearby for flare-ups.
Part 4

What Dads Actually Want

Skip the fancy brunch. This is the real list.

  • A clean garage or workshop. Not a sweater he'll never wear.
  • To watch the game without commentary. No asking who's winning every 2 minutes.
  • A nap. Uninterrupted, on the couch, with nobody jumping on him.
  • Meat. Good meat. Grill it for him, don't make him do it.
  • No plans. The best Father's Day is the one with nowhere to be.
Part 5

Quick Adventure Ideas

  • Fishing trip: Even if you don't catch anything, kids love being on the water.
  • Mini golf: Dad vs. kids. He'll let them win. Probably.
  • Build something: A birdhouse, a raised garden bed, a shelf. Kids help with the fun parts.
  • Drive-in movie: If there's one nearby. Bring blankets and snacks.
Part 6

Common Questions

What if dad says "I don't want anything"?▾
He means it. Don't force a big production. A card, a cookout, and a quiet day is perfect. The worst Father's Day is the one where he has to perform gratitude.
Should we celebrate stepdads or grandpas separately?▾
Ask. Some families do a combined cookout, others do separate calls or visits. The goal is that everyone who matters feels included — not that you run a schedule.
What if dad is not around?▾
Father's Day can be for any father figure: grandpa, uncle, stepdad, or a family friend who steps up. Celebrate whoever is in your kid's life.
